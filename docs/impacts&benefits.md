# SLIDE 5: Impact & Benefits

### Target audience & impact

- **SOC analysts**: less manual pcap triage, high-precision alerts only.
- **Network engineers**: zero-risk deployment (out-of-band), a concrete bandwidth-cost number before enabling any countermeasure.
- **Compliance auditors**: automated NIST-based scoring, one-click PDF/CSV reports for audit tickets.
- **CISOs / agency leadership (NTRO/CERT-In)**: measurable, defensible answer to "are we leaking information through our encrypted tunnels" — something no current tool provides.

### Benefits

- **Economic**: automates a manual compliance audit process that typically takes several hours per gateway — meaningfully reduces recurring Tier-3 engineering hours at scale **[ILLUSTRATIVE — state as a sourced industry-benchmark-based estimate if asked, not a measured figure]**.
- **Social / national security**: strengthens protection of government and critical-infrastructure communications against passive traffic-analysis by adversaries, without requiring payload decryption — so it doesn't create the privacy/DPDP tension a decrypting solution would.
- **Strategic / policy alignment**: an open, indigenously built tool for a defense use case supports self-reliance in national cybersecurity tooling rather than dependency on proprietary foreign hardware-based solutions (e.g., Cisco ETA).
- **Environmental (honest, non-forced angle)**: a lightweight, passive, software-based sensor avoids the need for additional proprietary hardware appliances at every gateway — a smaller physical/hardware footprint than hardware-dependent commercial alternatives.

**Visual concept — "Ripple Effect Map"** (not a stakeholder box grid): Concentric circles expanding outward from a small center icon (a single SOC analyst) — Ring 1: "Organization" (CISO/compliance), Ring 2: "Sector" (defense/critical infrastructure), Ring 3: "Nation" (digital sovereignty, Atmanirbhar Bharat in cybersecurity tooling). Label each ring with its specific, honestly-tagged benefit. This visually communicates *scale of impact* — a core judging criterion — far more effectively than four disconnected boxes.  **this slide directly covers both judging criteria**, and the structure is actually very well aligned with them.

## But I'd make 3 wording corrections before finalizing

### 1. Remove “zero-risk”

The Network Engineer card says:

> **Zero-risk (out-of-band)**

Change it to:

> **Passive, non-inline deployment**

It's technically more defensible. Don't give a judge an easy opportunity to challenge the absolute claim.

### 2. Don't say “something no current tool provides”

Unless you have a comprehensive competitive analysis proving that, change:

> “something no current tool provides”

to:

> **“a measurable view of metadata leakage and mitigation effectiveness.”**

Your novelty is stronger when you **demonstrate it** rather than make a sweeping market claim.

### 3. Make the economic claim explicitly illustrative

You already correctly marked it as illustrative in your source material. Keep that discipline on the slide:

> **Economic — Lower recurring audit effort**
> *Illustrative benefit; quantify after workflow benchmarking.*

Don't put “several hours per gateway” as though you experimentally measured it unless you actually did.
